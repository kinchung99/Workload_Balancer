Recording it

Getting a clean phone recording

  • Best: open the live URL on a real phone, screen-record, then drop it into a phone frame in editing. Most convincing — it is obviously a phone.
  • Easiest: desktop Chrome → DevTools → device toolbar → iPhone 14 Pro. Record that region only.
  • Hide the URL bar and bookmarks. A visible browser chrome makes it read as a website, not an app.
  • Move the cursor slowly and deliberately. Fast cursors read as nervous.

Before you hit record

  • Open Areas → Reset to the seeded week. Every number in this script assumes the seeded state.
  • Check the app reads 47% on Home. If it doesn't, you reset wrong.
  • Turn off notifications. One Slack banner costs you a retake.
  • Record narration separately from the screen. Trying to talk and demo at once is why most submissions sound hesitant.

Captions — do not skip these

Assume a panel watches at least part of it muted. Burn in captions (CapCut, Premiere or YouTube auto-captions corrected by hand). Beyond the full subtitles, put these seven phrases on screen as large text at the moment you say them — they are the takeaways you want remembered:

Delivery

  • Smile on the first line. It is audible, and it buys you the whole first segment.
  • Land the three jokes and then move on. Do not pause for a laugh that isn't in the room.
  • Slow down on numbers. "Forty-seven percent." "Eight days out." "Three hundred and thirteen."
  • Full stop = full stop. Short sentences are already written in; just honour them.
  • Never say "um, so basically". Re-record the line instead — it costs eight seconds.
  • Last line: slower and quieter. Confidence at the end reads as conviction.

If you are over 4:50 — cut in this order

  1. Friends (3:12, −14s) — the only segment nothing else depends on.
  2. "And yes, the battery has a face. It's tired too." (−5s) — lovely, not load-bearing.
  3. The Notes-app joke (−7s) — keep "writing the message is" and drop the aside.
  4. Tonight, down to one sentence (−10s) — keep Priority; it is the stronger of the pair.

Never cut: the hook, the dread drag at 0:22, the five dials, or "313 automated checks". Those four carry Clarity, Novelty and Feasibility between them.

Five ways teams lose Presentation marks

  1. Running over. The limit is stated in the rubric; 5:01 is a scored penalty. Export, watch, check the runtime.
  2. Explaining the ideation. The README is marked for that. In the video it is 60 seconds you cannot afford.
  3. Narrating the UI. "Now I click here, and this opens…" Say what it means; let the screen show the click.
  4. A slide deck with no product. You have a live app. Nearly half this video is it working — that is your advantage, use it.
  5. Trailing off. End on the closing line and a held logo. No "…so, yeah, thanks."
